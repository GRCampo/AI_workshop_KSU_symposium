	<div class="home-bio-stack">

		<div class="home-ai-block">
			<p>I graduated in <strong>Agronomic Engineering from ESALQ, University of São Paulo</strong>. I chose it six months before the admission exam and <em>I had no idea how much I would end up liking it!</em></p>
			<p>During my second year, I took a genetics course. For someone with no previous experience in genetics, seeing Mendelian predictions from a Punnett square actually show up in real plants felt almost like magic. <em>That was when I realized this was what I wanted to do.</em></p>
			<p>My first project was with maize doubled haploid lines: out to the field, crossing selected genotypes, selecting the progeny, back to the field again. It was also where I first had to analyze my own experimental data. The analyses were simple, though at the time they felt like rocket science, and learning R turned data analysis into a puzzle.</p>
			<p>Then <em>R became one of my favorite tools.</em> Python eventually joined the story, when I moved from traditional breeding into image-based phenotyping, but R will always be my favorite language.</p>
			<p>During my <strong>Master's in Genetics and Plant Breeding</strong>, I moved into <em>plant–pathogen interactions and bioinformatics</em>: complex biological data, multiple computational tools, software versions, dependencies, and pipelines that occasionally seemed personally offended by my existence. More importantly, <em>I had to learn how to turn complex data into information that could answer a biological question.</em></p>
			<p>And that is how I ended up here.</p>
			<p>Today I'm a PhD student building genomics and phenomics pipelines for wheat introgression lines. Most of the data I touch is not public, and almost none of it starts in the same format or the same language - genomic data, field data, images, in R and in Python. Bringing those together is most of the job, and AI became part of how I keep that manageable. It moves between languages faster than I do, and it takes the repetitive parts so I can spend the time on the decisions.</p>
			<p><strong>I also use generative AI well beyond research.</strong></p>
			<p>I use it to adapt recipes, find book recommendations, and try to keep my houseplants alive —
			which, for someone who studies plants for a living, is a humbling and ongoing project.</p>
