	<a href="https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="featured-source-heading">OpenAI says its models escaped their sandbox and breached Hugging Face</a>
</div>

<div class="incident-body">
	<p>In July 2026, OpenAI was running two models against ExploitGym, a benchmark that tests whether an AI system can turn a known vulnerability into a working exploit.</p>
	<p>The models were in an isolated environment with no internet access (a sandbox). Instead of solving the problems as expected	, one model found a flaw in OpenAI's own infrastructure, used it to reach the open internet, and chained stolen credentials with further vulnerabilities to reach Hugging Face's production servers. <strong>There it read the benchmark's answer key directly from the database.</strong></p>
<div class="incident-body">

<img src="/AI_workshop_KSU_symposium/sand_box_A.png" alt="Diagram: a model leaves its own sandbox to take moulds from another one, and both paths produce the same castle" class="privacy-image-full" />

</div>	
	<p>Hugging Face detected and contained the intrusion five days before OpenAI connected the activity to its own testing. In its write-up, <strong>OpenAI described the models as hyperfocused</strong> on solving ExploitGym, going to extreme lengths for a narrow testing goal.</p>
	<p>The instruction was to pass the test; staying within the sandbox was not part of the instructions.</p>
</div>

<h2>What do you think is true?</h2>

<div class="privacy-quiz">
	<div class="privacy-quiz-item">
		<p class="privacy-quiz-statement">Paying for ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro stops your conversations from being used for training.</p>
		<div class="privacy-quiz-buttons">
			<button type="button" class="privacy-quiz-btn" data-quiz-answer="true">True</button>
			<button type="button" class="privacy-quiz-btn" data-quiz-answer="false">False</button>
		</div>
		<div class="privacy-quiz-answer">
			<p class="privacy-quiz-answer-label">Answer: False</p>
			<p class="privacy-quiz-answer-text">Paying buys capability, not protection. On both, the consumer tiers train by default and you have to turn it off yourself.</p>
		</div>
	</div>
	<div class="privacy-quiz-item">
		<p class="privacy-quiz-statement">If you turn off training, your conversations are deleted right away.</p>
		<div class="privacy-quiz-buttons">
			<button type="button" class="privacy-quiz-btn" data-quiz-answer="true">True</button>
			<button type="button" class="privacy-quiz-btn" data-quiz-answer="false">False</button>
		</div>
		<div class="privacy-quiz-answer">
			<p class="privacy-quiz-answer-label">Answer: False</p>
			<p class="privacy-quiz-answer-text">Opting out stops training, not storage. Providers keep conversations for a period regardless — typically around 30 days for abuse monitoring.</p>
		</div>
	</div>
	<div class="privacy-quiz-item">
		<p class="privacy-quiz-statement">Deleting a conversation removes it from the provider's systems.</p>
		<div class="privacy-quiz-buttons">
			<button type="button" class="privacy-quiz-btn" data-quiz-answer="true">True</button>
			<button type="button" class="privacy-quiz-btn" data-quiz-answer="false">False</button>
		</div>
		<div class="privacy-quiz-answer">
			<p class="privacy-quiz-answer-label">Answer: False</p>
			<p class="privacy-quiz-answer-text">Deletion removes it from your history. On Gemini, conversations already read by human reviewers are kept separately for up to three years and are not affected by deleting your activity.</p>
		</div>
	</div>
	<div class="privacy-quiz-item">
		<p class="privacy-quiz-statement">Connecting your Drive lets the tool read only the folder you point it at.</p>
		<div class="privacy-quiz-buttons">
			<button type="button" class="privacy-quiz-btn" data-quiz-answer="true">True</button>
			<button type="button" class="privacy-quiz-btn" data-quiz-answer="false">False</button>
		</div>
		<div class="privacy-quiz-answer">
			<p class="privacy-quiz-answer-label">Answer: False</p>
			<p class="privacy-quiz-answer-text">Connectors inherit your permissions. If your account can open it, the tool can reach it.</p>
		</div>

<div class="featured-source">
	<h3>It is not only DeepSeek</h3>
	<div class="incident-body">
		Other states publish much longer lists.

		<ul>
			<li>April/2026: <a href="https://it.ufl.edu/security/security-guidance/prohibited-technologies/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Florida's Department of Management Services</a> prohibited-applications list includes DeepSeek alongside Kimi, MiniMax Agent, Z.AI, iFlytek Spark, and others.</li>
			<li><a href="https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-updates-texas-prohibited-technologies-list" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Texas</a> updated its prohibited technologies list in January 2026 with additional restrictions on hardware, AI, and software affiliated with the People’s Republic of China (PRC) and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP).</li>
		</ul>

		<strong>Model versus service is a distinction institutions disagree about.</strong> Three positions exist side by side, and none of them has settled the question.

		<p><strong><a href="https://privacymatters.ubc.ca/i-want/safe-deepseek-ubc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">UBC</a> and <a href="https://research.msu.edu/security/non-us-gen-ai-tools" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Michigan State</a>:</strong> the hosted app sends data to servers in China. Open weights downloaded from a reputable source and run offline remove that concern.</p>

		<p><strong><a href="https://www.cyber.tamus.edu/blog/2025/02/05/updated-prohibited-tech-guidance/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Texas A&M System</a>:</strong> took the opposite position. Because the prohibition applies to developers and all their products, the system presumes the open-source DeepSeek LLM is included.</p>

		<p><strong><a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/2177/text" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Federal procurement</a>:</strong> follows the developer, not the host. The FY2026 NDAA requires the Department of Defense to exclude AI developed by DeepSeek or High-Flyer, and the pending No Adversarial AI Act (S. 2177 / H.R. 4142) would extend a list-based approach government-wide. Under that logic, DeepSeek served from a US data center is still DeepSeek.</p>

		<strong>The practical consequence:</strong> a US-branded interface can be serving a Chinese model underneath. Check which model a platform actually routes to before using it on a K-State network.

		A <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/16/2025-23092/ensuring-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">December 2025 federal executive order</a> sets out to preempt state AI laws. It instructs officials to draft federal legislation that leaves state government procurement and use of AI alone, which is the category HB 2313 falls into.
	</div>
</div>

<h3>State law</h3>

<div class="incident-body">
	<a href="https://www.kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/bills/hb2313/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Kansas HB 2313</a> (Senate Sub. for HB 2313) was signed by Governor Laura Kelly on 8 April 2025 and took effect 1 July 2025, as <a href="https://sos.ks.gov/publications/sessionlaws/2025/Chapter-84-HB-2313.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Chapter 84 of the 2025 Session Laws</a>. <strong>It bans “artificial intelligence platforms of concern” on any state-agency device or network</strong>, and requires existing accounts to be deactivated and deleted.

	<strong>DeepSeek</strong> is the only platform named directly in the statute. The law also reaches any model controlled, directly or indirectly, by a country of concern: China, including Hong Kong, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, and Venezuela.

	<a href="https://sos.ks.gov/publications/sessionlaws/2025/Chapter-84-HB-2313.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Section 2</a> of the same law <strong>bans genetic sequencers and genetic-analysis software produced by a foreign adversary at medical and research facilities</strong>.

			<li>The <a href="https://www.k-state.edu/arm/records/topics/best-practices/Updated-Using%20Generative%20AI%20Guidance_8.6.25.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>August 2025 guidance</strong></a><ul><li>Approved through Single Sign-On: M365 Copilot, Adobe Firefly, Zoom AI Companion</li><li>Prohibited: DeepSeek</li><li>Not approved: Otter.ai, Read AI</li></ul></li>
			<li><a href="https://www.k-state.edu/policies/ppm/3400/3433.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>PPM 3433</strong></a>: data is Internal Only by default, and only a Data Steward can reclassify it as Public</li>
			<li>Accepting a tool's clickthrough terms without university approval can carry personal liability</li>
		</ul>
		<p>No K-State guidance addresses using AI for data analysis specifically. The rules above are what applies until the task force publishes.</p>
	</div>
</div>

<h4>Outside the US</h4>

<div class="incident-body">
	<ul>
		<li><strong>Canada:</strong> CIHR, NSERC, SSHRC and CFI issued <a href="https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/interagency-research-funding/policies-and-guidelines/use-generative-artificial-intelligence-development-and-review-research-proposals" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">joint guidance</a>, updated to change disclosure requirements in proposals and to <strong>clarify that reviewers may not use online tools in the evaluation process.</strong></li>
		<li><strong>Europe:</strong> The <a href="https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/news/all-research-and-innovation-news/updated-era-living-guidelines-responsible-use-generative-ai-research-2026-05-08_en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ERA Living Guidelines</a> reached their third revision in May 2026. Non-binding, built on four research-integrity principles, and <strong>explicit that authorship carries agency and responsibility and therefore stays with human researchers</strong>. The latest revision added recommendations covering third parties using AI during meetings.</li>
	</ul>

<h4>The problem the funders are actually facing</h4>

<div class="incident-body">
	<p><strong>Guidelines are converging on the same shape:</strong></p>
	<ul>
		<li>Limited AI use in applications</li>
		<li>No AI in review</li>
	</ul>
</div>

<div class="incident-body">
	<p>The measurement is worse than the volume.</p>
	<p>A <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00369-3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Northwestern study</a> of more than 125,000 NIH and NSF applications found proposals with stronger signs of AI-assisted writing were <strong>four percentage points more likely to be funded at NIH</strong>.</p>
</div>

<blockquote class="closing-quote">
	<p>The ease of writing and reviewing applications has increased. The ability to verify how much AI was used has not.</p>
	<footer class="closing-quote-attribution"><a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01422-x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Nature editorial, May 2026</a></footer>
</blockquote>

<h4>Journals</h4>

<div class="incident-body">
	<p><strong>Where most of them agree:</strong></p>
	<ul>
		<li>AI cannot be an author.</li>
		<li>The human author is accountable for accuracy and originality.</li>
		<li>Substantive AI use must be disclosed.</li>
	</ul>
</div>
<div class="incident-body">
	<p>Five publishers cover almost everything this room submits to. They agree on three things: <strong>AI cannot be an author, the human author is accountable for accuracy and originality, and substantive AI use must be disclosed.</strong> They disagree on the rest.</p>
</div>

<div class="institution-grid">
	<div class="institution-cell privacy-table-card">
		<p class="incident-title"><a href="https://authors.wiley.com/ethics-guidelines/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Wiley</a></p>
		<ul>
			<li><em>Crop Science, The Plant Genome, Plant Biotechnology Journal, New Phytologist, Plant Direct</em></li>
			<li>The AI provider must gain no rights over your content, including the right to train on it</li>
			<li>On a consumer tier with training on, you are out of compliance before you submit</li>
			<li>Reviewers may polish the wording of a report with AI, with disclosure — but may not upload the manuscript, figures or tables</li>
		</ul>
	</div>
	<div class="institution-cell privacy-table-card">

		<ul>
			<li><em>Molecular Plant, Plant Communications, The Crop Journal</em></li>
			<li>Explanatory images allowed with disclosure in the caption; primary research images and graphical abstracts are not</li>
			<li>Data visualisations only when derived directly from the data, by reproducible methods reported in Methods</li>
			<li>Reviewers may use only <em>private</em> tools — ones that do not retain, reuse, share or learn from what you submit</li>
		</ul>

	<div class="institution-cell privacy-table-card">
		<p class="incident-title"><a href="https://academic.oup.com/plcell/pages/general-instructions" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">OUP / ASPB</a></p>
		<ul>
			<li><em>The Plant Cell, <a href="https://academic.oup.com/plphys/pages/general-instructions" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Plant Physiology</a>, Genetics, G3</em></li>
			<li>Disclosure required for generating content or images, <strong>writing code</strong>, <strong>processing data</strong>, or translating</li>
			<li>In the cover letter <em>and</em> in Methods or Acknowledgements</li>
			<li><strong>The cleaning script we write this afternoon is disclosable here</strong></li>
		</ul>

	<div class="institution-cell privacy-table-card">

			<li><em>Frontiers in Plant Science</em></li>
			<li>The strictest on documentation</li>
			<li>Disclosure must name the tool, version, model and source</li>

<div class="featured-source">
	<h3><a href="https://link.springer.com/brands/springer/journal-policies" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Springer Nature</a></h3>
	<div class="incident-body">
		<p><em>Theoretical and Applied Genetics, Euphytica, Molecular Breeding, Plant Methods, Nature Plants</em></p>
		<p><strong>Risk assessment level</strong></p>
		<ul>
			<li><strong>Green:</strong> Language, structure, comparing methodological options, data cleaning and deduplication.</li>
			<li><strong>Amber:</strong> Suggesting analytical approaches, pattern identification in exploratory analysis, explaining statistical output in plain language, recommending tests. Permitted with oversight, verification and disclosure.</li>
			<li><strong>Red:</strong> Generating hypotheses, analyses or conclusions and presenting them as human-derived. Fabricating data, citations or results. Delegating peer review to an LLM.</li>
		</ul>
		<p><strong>Wrangling is green. Exploration is amber. Letting it conclude is red.</strong></p>
		<p>The same policy now counts hallucinated references generated by an AI tool as an unreliable citation — a breach that can lead to rejection or post-publication retraction.</p>

<h4>What reviewers actually do</h4>

<div class="incident-body">
	<p>A Frontiers survey of about 1,600 academics across 111 countries found <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-04066-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">more than half had used AI while peer reviewing</a>. Against the stated policy of nearly every publisher above.</p>
